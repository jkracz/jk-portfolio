# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Recruiters, founders, and engineering leaders at startups and corporations who are evaluating Joe Kracz for one of three tracks: full-time engineering roles, fractional or advisory engagements, or AI / software contracting work. They land here in evaluation mode — they have a problem (or an opening) and ten tabs open. They are technically literate, skeptical of marketing language, and looking for evidence that Joe ships real product, not just resume keywords.

The job to be done: in under a minute, decide whether Joe is worth a conversation. Within a few minutes, find one piece of work credible enough to forward to a stakeholder.

## Product Purpose

joekracz.com is a personal brand surface that does double duty as a portfolio and a hire-me page. It exists to convert "someone mentioned Joe" or a cold landing into a qualified inbound — for FT roles, fractional/advisory engagements, or project contracts — by demonstrating that Joe is a capable, senior product builder across the stack with real shipped work behind him.

Eventually the contracting half will move to a separate consulting site and joekracz.com will narrow to portfolio + writing. Today it carries both.

Success looks like: a recruiter or founder leaves with a clear mental model of what Joe builds, why hiring him is low-risk, and a frictionless path to contact.

## Brand Personality

Confident, playful, technical, human.

- **Confident** — opinions stated plainly, no hedging, no "passionate about" filler. Senior tone.
- **Playful** — moments of personality and craft that signal taste. Not zany, not theme-park.
- **Technical** — speaks the dialect of engineers and product people, doesn't water it down for marketing.
- **Human** — first person, real photo, a person you'd want to work with, not a corporate brochure.

Voice rules: short declarative sentences. Specific over generic. Show the work, don't claim the trait.

## Anti-references

The site must explicitly NOT look or read like:

- **The "hacker" terminal** — black background with neon green/red, ASCII art, fake CRT scanlines, monospaced everything. Trying-too-hard cliché.
- **SaaS-cream gradient hero** — pastel-purple-to-pink hero gradient, a big rounded CTA, three identical feature cards. Indistinguishable from every Vercel-template site.
- **"Creative developer" WebGL playground** — 3D blob shaders, scroll-jacked physics, audio-reactive everything. Reads as performance, not capability.
- **Agency marquee maximalism** — oversized scrolling text, "WE BUILD ★ WE SHIP ★ WE CARE ★", neo-brutalist sticker collages. Loud, indistinguishable, dated fast.
- **Generic dev-portfolio "Hi, I'm X" template** — same Tailwind starter everyone uses, a tech-icon wall, a timeline of jobs. Zero point of view.

## Design Principles

1. **Practice what you preach.** The site is itself a portfolio piece. Anything sloppy here implies sloppiness in the work. Craft is non-negotiable.
2. **Restraint reads as seniority.** Take Linear's posture: quiet, confident, precise. Loud sites compensate; this one doesn't need to.
3. **Playfulness lives in the details, not the silhouette.** Layout, motion, and small surprises (à la kenjimmy.xyz) carry the personality. The overall structure stays calm and scannable.
4. **Show the work, not the adjective.** Real projects, real outcomes, real code. Replace any sentence that could appear on a hundred other portfolios.
5. **One screen, one decision.** Each section answers exactly one question a visitor has (Who is this? What does he build? Has he shipped? Why him? How do I reach him?). No section earns a second purpose.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Reasonable defaults: WCAG 2.1 AA target. Color contrast meets AA in both themes. Honor `prefers-reduced-motion` for the typewriter, hero animations, and any scroll-driven motion. Keyboard reachable, focus rings visible, semantic headings. No accessibility-blocking effects (e.g. transparent text over busy imagery, motion that can't be paused).
